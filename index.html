CASE FILE WR-09

A Surprise Before the Ceremony

Elena and Damon were about to be married. At 4.30 p.m., when it was time to exchange rings, the best man, Adrien, opened the ring box and discovered that the wedding ring was missing.

A strange note had also reached Ms Green, the wedding officiant. Was it a genuine warning, or an attempt to blame someone else?

Your task: Identify who took the ring, where it was taken and what the reversed note says.

Investigation rules

Exactly one of the four suspects deliberately stole the ring, acting alone. It was not accidentally misplaced or dropped. The confirmed recordings and observations are accurate. Suspect statements and the note may be misleading.

The ring was confirmed present at 4.17 p.m. and confirmed missing at 4.20 p.m. It did not leave the box before that interval. Each suspect held one different listed object at 4.19 p.m.; each object must be used once in the grid.

This is a fictional wedding mystery. An argument or a fingerprint on a shared pen alone does not establish guilt.

Timeline & Evidence

Clue 1 · 4.03–4.07 p.m. · A camera fault

The groom’s room camera briefly failed, then resumed recording. The later confirmed sighting of the ring at 4.17 p.m. rules out this outage as the time of the theft.

Clue 2 · 4.04–4.06 p.m. · The pouch

Adrien collected a small pouch from the groom’s room. A confirmed inspection later showed it held cufflinks, not the ring. His possession of a pouch alone does not prove theft.

Clue 3 · 4.05–4.08 p.m. · Lily’s visit

Lily briefly visited the groom’s room. The ring was subsequently confirmed present at 4.17 p.m., so it was not stolen during this visit.

Clue 4 · 4.17 p.m. · The ring is present

The wedding organiser opened the ring box in the groom’s room and confirmed the ring was inside. She then closed the box and left it on the table. The organiser left the room empty.

Clue 5 · 4.18–4.20 p.m. · Who could reach the box?

Continuous footage showed Eleanor and Adrien entering the groom’s room. Eleanor was the only person to open or touch the ring box before 4.20 p.m. Adrien stood by the doorway, never reached the box and did not receive anything from Eleanor.

Clue 6 · A complete view

The recording covered the ring box and all approaches to it throughout the interval. No other person entered, no one moved the table, and no other route to the box existed. The thief took the ring directly from the box.

Clue 7 · 4.19 p.m. · Objects in hand

The room camera showed Eleanor writing with the used pen while Adrien held the small pouch. At the same time, hall footage showed Ms Green holding the ceremony folder and corridor footage showed Lily holding her flower basket.

Clue 8 · 4.20 p.m. · The empty box

The organiser returned just as Eleanor and Adrien left and checked the box again. The ring was gone. She immediately alerted staff; the later 4.30 p.m. announcement took place while the search continued.

Clue 9 · The note’s journey

Eleanor folded the note she had written and handed it to Lily. Lily delivered the same folded sheet to Ms Green without changing it. The verified handover record showed nobody replaced it. It read: “neirdA elots eht gnir”.

Clue 10 · The shared pen

Only Adrien’s and Lily’s fingerprints were recovered from the pen. Both had used it earlier. Eleanor was recorded writing with that pen while wearing gloves. Fingerprints cannot establish who wrote a particular message or when the pen was touched.

Clue 11 · The discarded gloves

Gloves were found in a bin, but investigators could not link that discarded pair to a suspect. Their presence alone does not identify the thief.

Clue 12 · The disagreement

Eleanor’s recent argument with Elena provides possible background, not proof. The decisive evidence is the ring’s confirmed presence and the record of access to its box.

Teacher Case Solution

Eleanor · Groom’s room · A misleading accusation against Adrien

The ring was inside the box at 4.17 p.m. and missing at 4.20 p.m. During that complete interval, only Eleanor accessed the box. Under the stated single-thief and direct-removal rules, she is the only possible thief.

Reversing each word gives “Adrien stole the ring”. The verified note handovers show Eleanor supplied it, but the complete access record rules Adrien out. The accusation is misleading.

The earlier CCTV outage and visits happened before the ring’s last confirmed sighting. The pen fingerprints show earlier contact, not authorship. Eleanor’s recorded use of gloves explains why her prints need not appear. The unlinked discarded gloves cannot identify anyone.

Ending: confronted with the evidence, Eleanor returns the ring to the organiser. The couple can proceed with their ceremony. Her return of the ring is the ending, not a clue needed to solve the case.
